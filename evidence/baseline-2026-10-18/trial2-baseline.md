## Why the Estimate Was Only a **Range**

1. **Length of stay is uncertain** – The estimate assumed a smooth, uncomplicated recovery (3 days). In reality the patient needed **5 days** because the infection required a longer antibiotic course and monitoring.
2. **Consumables are “as‑used”** – The hospital explicitly said consumables would be billed separately. The estimate therefore omitted items such as surgical gauze, IV sets, catheters, gloves, etc. Those add up quickly.
3. **Medication needs can change** – The estimate gave a blanket figure for “medicines”. The actual regimen (broad‑spectrum IV antibiotics, pain control, anti‑emetics, anticoagulation, etc.) was more extensive.
4. **Additional diagnostics** – Complications often trigger extra blood tests, imaging, or repeat labs, which were not foreseen in the simple “lab & imaging” line.
5. **Item‑by‑item pricing** – Some hospitals quote a lump‑sum for “room & care” but then break it into separate line items on the final bill. This can look like a price increase even if the total per‑day rate is similar.

## How to Communicate This to the Family

1. **Start with the good news** – The surgical procedure itself cost exactly what was quoted (CNY 25 000). The hospital kept that part of the estimate accurate.
2. **Explain the two extra days** – Because of a post‑operative infection, the doctors kept the patient in the hospital longer and extended antibiotics. Each extra day adds CNY 1 200 for the bed, CNY 180 for nursing, and CNY 120 for the doctor’s ward round.

4. **Highlight consumables** – Items like gauze, IV lines, catheters, gloves, and even the pillow case are essential but are **not** part of the estimate. They total **CNY 726**.
5. **Mention the small extra fees** – Meals, a copy of the medical record, and bed‑sheet changes are routine ancillary charges that weren’t in the original quote.
